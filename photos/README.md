# Component photos

Drop photos here and tell Claude. Any format, any filename, though a descriptive
name helps (`display-front.jpg` beats `IMG_4821.jpg`).

## The shots that matter most

In rough order of how much they unblock:

1. **The display**, front and back. The back is the important one: part numbers and
   pin labels are usually printed there. This is the biggest open question, because
   the driver depends on which chip it is.
2. **Your jumper wires.** A shot of the ends is enough. Whether they are male-to-male
   or female-to-male decides how the shield connects without soldering.
3. **The LDRs.** Mostly to confirm how many you have, which decides whether tonight
   is a one-beam or two-beam test.
4. **The buzzer**, close enough to read any printed text. Active and passive buzzers
   look identical but behave differently: the firmware uses `tone()`, which needs a
   passive one. If it is active it will beep at one fixed pitch no matter what.
5. **A shield, top side**, square on. Confirms the spare prototyping holes and lets
   the pin labels be read.
6. **The resistor collection.** Band colours are hard to photograph well, so shoot
   straight on in bright light, or just photograph the labelled bags or card.
7. **Anything you cannot identify.** That is exactly what this folder is for.

## Taking a usable photo of small parts

- Daylight or a bright lamp, no flash. Flash blows out printed text.
- Plain background: a sheet of white paper or a dark tea towel.
- Fill the frame. Get close, then crop. A part taking up a tenth of the frame is
  usually unreadable.
- Printed text square on, not at an angle.
- Lay a coin or a ruler beside anything whose size matters.
- Phones focus badly up close. If it will not focus, back off and crop instead.
